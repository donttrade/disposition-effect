How it works

What is counted, and what is not

Pick a day and the page sets it beside your typical day: the number of closed trades, their net result, the largest single loss, and four counts of what happened around losses. Every row is a count or a sum from your file. Nothing is tested or called a mistake.

  • A trade counts on the day it closed, after the time shift you choose.
  • Your typical day is, for each row, the median of your other days with trades. It is shown only when the file has at least 10 other days; with fewer, a median says little.
  • Re-entries: trades opened within 15 minutes after a losing trade closed. Trades after 2 or more losses in a row: trades opened when the last trade closed before them was the second or later loss in a row. Bigger size after a loss: the last trade closed before it was a loss and the size is 1.5 times the symbol’s median size or more. Losses over 2 times your median loss: losing trades larger than twice the median of all your losses.
  • A file with dates but no times of day cannot show re-entries or sizes after a loss, and the page leaves those rows out.
  • A day with many losses will show more trades after losses almost by construction. The counts describe that day; they do not say why it went as it did.

Questions

Why did you build this for 5 November 2026?

It was a sharp day for stocks, and for the builder-listed markets on one crypto exchange. We counted forced closes there in a study whose method was written down first. This page lets you look at your own account that day, privately.

Does a high count mean I did something wrong?

No. The counts describe what is in your file. A day with many losses will show more trades after losses almost by construction. The page does not judge a day and does not tell you what to do.

Why is there no typical day?

The file has fewer than 10 other days with trades. Add an earlier export, or read the day’s counts on their own.
