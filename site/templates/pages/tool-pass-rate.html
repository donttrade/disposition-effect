    Common mistakes

    • Mixing cohorts, such as dividing this month’s passes by this month’s starts. Those passes mostly came from earlier starts.
    • Dropping open accounts. They are neither passes nor failures yet, so show the range.
    • Counting resets and retries as new people. They are new accounts, but not new traders.
    • Quoting the payout rate per funded account as if it were per evaluation. The two use different denominators.

    What this calculator can’t do

    It checks the arithmetic. It cannot tell anyone your inputs are right: a rate a firm computes about itself is still the firm’s own claim.

    Our paid work measures something no pass rate shows: how your traders handle winning and losing trades, computed from your own records by someone outside the firm, with the method published.

    Questions

    How is a prop-firm pass rate calculated?

    Among finished evaluations in one cohort: passed ÷ (started − still open). A cohort is everything that started in one fixed window. Open accounts are neither passes nor failures yet, so the page also shows the range if every open one fails or passes.

    Why does the cohort matter?

    Dividing this month’s passes by this month’s starts mixes cohorts, because those passes mostly came from earlier starts. Use the same cohort for every number.

    What is the difference between the payout rate per evaluation and per funded account?

    Per evaluation is the accounts paid out ÷ the evaluations started. Per funded account is the accounts paid out ÷ the accounts that passed. The two use different denominators, so they are not interchangeable.

    Is my data sent anywhere?

    No. The calculator runs in your browser and nothing you type is sent.

    Is this investment advice?

    No. It checks arithmetic on the counts you enter and does not recommend any security, trade, size or exit.
