The formula, and why the gain is larger than the fall

If an account falls by a fraction d of its high, it keeps 1 − d of it. To get back to the high, that smaller balance has to grow by the factor 1 ÷ (1 − d), which is a gain of d ÷ (1 − d). The gain is always larger than the fall, and the gap widens quickly as the fall deepens.

Maximum drawdown, current drawdown and duration

  • Maximum drawdown is the largest fall from a running high to a later low, in money or as a share of that high.
  • Current drawdown is how far below its running high the account is now. It is zero at a new high.
  • Drawdown duration is how long a drawdown lasted, from the high that began it to the trade that brought the account back to that high. One still open at the end of a file has no end yet.

A deep drawdown can be short and a shallow one long, so the three numbers answer different questions.

Why platforms report different drawdown figures

  • The baseline differs. A figure can be measured from the starting balance, from the highest balance so far, from the highest equity so far or from the start of the day.
  • Closed trades or equity. A file of closed trades shows what was realised. A platform that counts open positions can show a deeper dip that never appears in the closed-trade file.
  • Money or percent. The same fall is a different percentage depending on which balance it is measured against.
  • A firm’s rule is its own definition. Trailing and daily limits are set out in each firm’s published rules, and the Challenge Readiness Check takes them as rules you pick or enter.

Questions

How do you calculate the gain needed to recover from a drawdown?

Divide the fall by what is left: d ÷ (1 − d). A fall of 20% leaves 80% of the high, and the gain on that 80% that gets back to 100% is 20 ÷ 80, which is {{ gain_for(20) }}.

Why does a 50% drawdown need a 100% gain?

The account has half of its high left, and doubling half gets back to the whole. The table gives {{ gain_for(50) }} for a 50% fall.

What is the difference between maximum drawdown and drawdown duration?

Maximum drawdown is the depth of the largest fall from a running high. Duration is how long a fall lasted, from the high to the trade that brought the account back to it.

Is drawdown measured on closed trades or on equity?

Both are used. A file of closed trades gives the closed-trade drawdown. A platform or a firm’s rule can use equity that includes open positions, measured from the start of the day or from the highest balance. The figures differ because the baseline differs.

How long does a drawdown last?

It depends on what the account earns afterwards, which no table can say. The second table shows only the arithmetic for a steady gain per period, which real trading does not have.

Is this investment advice?

No. It is arithmetic and does not recommend any security, trade, size or exit. Past results do not predict future results.
