It measures and describes up to five habits in your own file, like opening a new trade soon after a loss, with the dollar totals of the matching trades, and tests up to two of these habits against chance (could the pattern be luck?) in this version. How the results work

Each tested habit gets one of three results: “distinguishable from noise”, “not distinguishable from noise”, or “not enough trades to tell” when either of the two groups it compares has fewer than 30 trades. Each test compares trades from the same days with each other. Dollar figures are historical totals from your file. They describe what happened; they do not show that a habit caused a result.

How it works

  1. Export your history. A MetaTrader 5 or 4 report, an IBKR Flex Query or a Tradovate orders export (all three in beta), or the donttrade CSV template.
  2. Your browser reads it. It removes names, account numbers and symbols. It sends our server only stripped trade numbers. Those are not kept; only a scrambled fingerprint of each trade is kept, for the re-test.
  3. Read your report. Run the free re-test on the same account 30 to 60 days after your first report.

Questions

Is this advice?

No. It describes your own past trades. It does not tell you what to trade, how much, or when to exit.

Where does my trade file go?

Your file stays in your browser. The free tools send nothing. For the paid report: Your browser sends our server only stripped trade numbers: open and close times, buy or sell, relative size, the realised result, a random symbol number and a scrambled fingerprint of each row. No name, account number or ticker is sent. The numbers are not kept; we keep only a scrambled fingerprint of each trade row from your first report, so a re-test can be matched to the same history, and we delete it on the dates in our privacy notice.

What does the re-test do?

It runs the same report on a newer export of the same account, 30 to 60 days after your first report, so you can compare the two. It is included in the price. Your re-test runs on the version of the report that is live when you run it; every change is dated on the corrections page.
