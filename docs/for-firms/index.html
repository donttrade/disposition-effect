Copy-trading platforms: the copy-leader persistence test

Sold to platforms only, never to followers. For copy-trading apps, social trading networks, crypto exchanges with copy features and vault front-ends.

Does your leader ranking predict what followers actually earn after copying costs and delay? The test answers that out of sample: leaders are ranked on one period and judged on a later period the ranking never saw. It is adjusted for luck, so a ranking counts only if it does better than chance would.

  • A sealed method. The ranking rule, the periods, the cost and delay assumptions and the pass mark are fixed in writing before anyone sees data, and they don’t change afterwards.
  • Run on your systems. Your engineers run a script we send with the scope, and can read every line of it first. Only aggregate results come back. We hold no data: no follower, leader or account record leaves your systems.
  • About your ranking, not about people. The result names no leader and scores no one.
  • Not advice. It is not a recommendation of any trader or leader, to you or to your followers, and no guarantee of future results. It is not an audit or a certification: please don’t call any leader or ranking audited, certified or verified.
  • Optional re-check. The same test on a fresh period, on the quarterly re-check terms above.

Firms that make behaviour claims: the claims substantiation test

We report the result whether it supports the claim or not. For trading journals, education sellers, prop firms and brokers whose marketing says their product changes how traders behave, such as “our journal makes you more disciplined”.

Does the claim hold on your own users’ records? Before anyone sees data, the claim is written down as a test: which behaviour, which users, which periods, what comparison, and what result would support it. That test is sealed (fingerprinted with a SHA-256 hash) and then run once, as written.

  • Pre-registered and sealed. The measure, the comparison and the pass mark are fixed in writing before anyone sees data, and they don’t change afterwards.
  • Run on your systems. Your engineers run a script we send with the scope, and can read every line of it first. Only aggregate results come back; no user record leaves your systems.
  • Either answer is a finished result. We report the result whether it supports the claim or not. The fee is the same either way, and you decide whether anything is published.
  • Limits stated. We tell you in the scope, before you pay, whether your records can test the claim at all, and what the design can and cannot show. A before-and-after comparison, for example, cannot by itself show that your product caused a change.
  • Not a certification, a verification or an audit. It is a test, with its result and its limits. Please don’t call the claim or the product certified, verified or audited.

What your staff export

Five columns, one row per open position per day, plus a row on the day it closes. One position, as rows, from the export guide:

HOW TO READ THE AGE-CONTROLLED FIGURES
- Positions are sold less often the longer they are held. If losing
  positions are on average older than winning ones, the pooled ratio at
  the top can show an effect that is only an age pattern. The within-age
  ratio compares winners and losers of the same holding age; use it, not
  the pooled ratio, for the size of the effect.
- Holding age is counted from each position's first row in this file.
  Positions already open on the file's first date are left out of these
  figures because their age is unknown; the pooled figures still use them.
- The fixed-effects line compares each account with itself, at the same
  holding day. It is a different average from the pooled ratio, so the two
  are not expected to match.
- Gain timing: a position's gain or loss on the day it is sold may partly
  reflect the sale itself (the fill or the closing mark). The lines that
  take the gain or loss from the position's previous row avoid that. If
  they are much smaller, the effect depends on same-day timing.
- Not ruled out: traders may sell large moves in either direction more
  often (a V-shaped pattern, Ben-David and Hirshleifer 2012). Separating
  that from a preference for selling winners needs the size of each gain
  or loss relative to the position, which these five columns do not carry.
- The intervals here are clustered by account (the within-age ratio) and
  by account and date (fixed effects). The t statistic at the top treats
  every row as independent and is not.
- NOT MEASURED means the data cannot support the figure, never zero.

Why a re-run number is harder to wave away

The Financial Commission’s prop-firm code took effect in July 2026. It asks for drawdown rules that are objective and verifiable, and certification runs on attestations: firms sign one to join and file quarterly attestations that the Commission can review. (Code of Conduct; FinanceFeeds)

Firms already pay for outside checks. Hola Prime commissioned Deloitte for an independent review of payout processing and published the result. That review covered operations, in a window the firm chose. It did not measure how traders behaved. (release)

A figure someone else computed, with the method published, can be checked by anyone who reads it.
