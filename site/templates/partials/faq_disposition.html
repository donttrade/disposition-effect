Questions

What is the disposition effect?

The tendency to sell positions that are up more readily than positions that are down. Shefrin and Statman (1985) named it, and Odean (1998) measured it on brokerage records.

How are PGR and PLR calculated?

PGR is realised gains ÷ (realised gains + paper gains), and PLR is realised losses ÷ (realised losses + paper losses). Following Odean (1998), the counts are added up over the days an account sold something.

What does a PGR to PLR ratio above 1 mean?

That in these counts gains were sold more readily than losses. The calculator shows the ratio, the difference and a t-statistic, and the page lists what the figure does not tell you, such as holding age.

Does the calculator test whether the result is chance?

It reports a t-statistic with Odean’s standard error, which treats every position as independent. That overstates how precise the result is when a few accounts hold most of the positions.

Is this investment advice?

No. It is arithmetic on counts you type in and does not recommend any security, trade, size or exit.
