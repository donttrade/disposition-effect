What’s free, what’s paid, and why

Free tools: $0

They run on your computer, so serving them costs us almost nothing. No account, no card, no time limit. They show what happened in your trades: totals, streaks and drawdowns, and one disposition figure from four counts you type in.

Habit report: $39 founding price for the first 100 buyers

The report reads the closed trades in your file: the latest 20,000 rows at most, leaving out trades opened more than about 10 years before your latest trade or held longer than 3,650 days. It measures and describes up to five habits, with the dollar totals of the matching trades, and tests up to two of them against chance in this version, comparing trades from the same days. It includes one re-test 30 to 60 days after your first report. Your re-test runs on the version of the report that is live when you run it; every change is dated on the corrections page. The tests run on our server on stripped trade numbers.

What you buy
Habit report: a report on habits in your own past trades, built from a trade file you choose. It measures and describes up to five habits, with the dollar totals of the matching trades, and tests up to two of them against chance in this version. It includes one re-test 30 to 60 days after your first report. It describes what happened; it does not tell you what to trade.
Technical requirements
A current version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. Files: MetaTrader 5 or 4 HTML report, Interactive Brokers Flex Query CSV or Tradovate Orders CSV (all three in beta until a real export confirms the columns), or the donttrade CSV template. Your browser reads the file. Your browser sends our server only stripped trade numbers: open and close times, buy or sell, relative size, the realised result, a random symbol number and a scrambled fingerprint of each row. No name, account number or ticker is sent. The numbers are not kept; we keep only a scrambled fingerprint of each trade row from your first report, so a re-test can be matched to the same history, and we delete it on the dates in our privacy notice.
Price
$39 USD, one time. No sales tax is charged. Total: $39 USD.
Currency and fees
Priced in US dollars. Stripe’s payment page may offer to charge you in your own currency instead, at Stripe’s exchange rate, which includes a 2–4% conversion fee. If you pay in US dollars, your card issuer may charge a currency conversion fee. donttrade receives neither fee.
Payment
Card, through Stripe's checkout page.
When you get it
Right after payment, in your browser. One re-test is included, to run 30 to 60 days after your first report. Your re-test runs on the version of the report that is live when you run it; every change is dated on the corrections page. Access lasts 90 days after purchase, or until 75 days after your first report if that is later.
Restrictions
Two runs: the report and one re-test of the same account (the re-test file has to share at least half of the first file's trades). At most 20,000 rows per file. A tested habit with fewer than 30 trades in either group is shown as "not enough trades to tell". If 5% or more of a file's rows open at exactly 00:00 and are held a whole number of days, as in exports with dates but no times, no habit is tested, and the habits that depend on the order of trades within a day (re-entering soon after a loss, size after a loss, trading after a losing run) are not described.

How the test works

Firms that make behaviour claims

The claims substantiation test: your marketing says your product changes how traders behave, and a pre-registered test, fingerprinted before any data is seen, checks whether that claim holds on your own users’ records. We report the result whether it supports the claim or not. It is a test with stated limits; it is not an audit. It uses the engagements above, at the same prices: the Pilot for one claim, one measure and one comparison (one per firm), a Snapshot for the same, a Memo for every measure and comparison agreed in the scope, and an Outcomes pack running the same pre-registered test again on a later period.

How payment works today

  • Every firm engagement is invoiced by email, in USD. International clients pay by bank transfer; clients in Canada may pay the CAD equivalent by Interac e-Transfer, at the Bank of Canada daily rate on the invoice date. Transfer and bank fees are paid by the sender.
  • The engagement agreement is sent with the scope, before you pay. A blank template is available on request.
  • The habit report is paid by card on Stripe’s own page, from the “Pay $39 on Stripe” button in the last step above.
  • For firm engagements: Card payments are accepted through a secure Stripe link, sent only after you accept the scope.
  • We never ask for card details by email.

Cancellations, refunds and the Pilot

  • Firms: no cancellation fees. The Pilot is refundable in full until data kick-off, the day your staff first run the script on your records. After that, it is refunded if your extract can’t be made to run, or through the reproduction refund. Snapshot, Memo and Outcomes pack are paid 50% on acceptance and 50% within 7 days of delivery; if either side stops, completed work is billed pro rata.
  • Traders: a full refund if you ask within 14 days of buying, even if you have already run your report.

Questions about price

Why publish prices?

So you know the cost before you write, and every firm pays the same.

Do you take card payments?

Yes. Card payments are accepted through a secure Stripe link, sent only after you accept the scope. Bank transfer, or Interac e-Transfer in Canada, also work.

What is the difference between the Pilot, a Snapshot and a Memo?

The Pilot is your first Snapshot at an introductory price, one per firm. A Snapshot is one figure for one population and period. A Memo adds your ratio set beside the public study’s, with the differences explained in writing.

Is the Pilot fee lost if we go ahead?

No. Credited in full toward a Memo signed within 60 days of delivery.

Can the price change after we accept?

No. It is fixed in writing in the scope you accept.

Does paying change the result?

No. Independence policy
