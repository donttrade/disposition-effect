How it works

How each replay works

The check builds 10,000 replays from your own record. Each replayed day is one of your past trading days, drawn at random (a day can come up more than once), with its trades in their real order. A day with three quick losses stays a day with three quick losses, which is what daily loss limits react to.

  1. After every trade, a replay breaks a rule if the day’s loss reaches the daily limit, or if the balance reaches the maximum-loss floor. Reaching a limit counts as breaking it.
  2. A moving floor follows the highest balance, at the end of each day or after each trade, and can stop at a level you set.
  3. A replay passes when the balance reaches the target (at the end of the day, or at once if you choose), with enough days counted and, with a best-day share, no single day above it. A replay held by a day count or a best-day share keeps going.
  4. A replay that neither passes nor breaks a rule within the time limit runs out of time.

“In their real order” replays your days once, as they happened. “Try a rule” runs the same replays again with one or two rules from a fixed list, so only the rule differs.

It runs entirely in your browser. Only anonymous counts of visits and finished steps are sent; your file is never sent.

Questions

Is this a prediction of whether I will pass?

No. It shows how your past trading days would have fared under the rules shown if they came in a different order. Future days can differ from past ones, and past results do not predict future results.

Why are whole days replayed instead of single trades?

Daily loss limits depend on how losses bunch within a day. Replaying whole days keeps that bunching, while drawing single trades would spread a bad morning across many days and make daily limits look safer than they were.

Which firms’ rules are in the list?

11 rule sets from 8 firms, as each firm publishes them, with the date they were read and a link to the firm’s rules page. A value a firm’s page does not state is marked “not stated by the firm” and runs with the default shown. Rules change and vary by account type, so every box stays editable.

What does “Habits on breach days” mean?

Four habits, with the same definitions as the donttrade test, counted on the days a daily limit broke and on your other losing days. It counts how often a habit was there, not whether it caused anything.

How many trading days do I need in the file?

The check runs with any number, but with fewer than 30 trading days the result leans heavily on a few days. More history gives a steadier picture.

Is my file uploaded?

No. The file is read in this tab and all 10,000 replays happen in your browser. Only anonymous counts of visits and finished steps are sent; your file is never sent.
