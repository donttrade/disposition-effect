Do early trading habits predict prop-challenge rule breaches?

We take each account’s first 30 trades and compute the donttrade habit score (DT-1) from them. Then we ask whether accounts with higher scores went on to break a daily-loss or max-loss rule in the next 30 days more often than accounts with lower scores, and whether the score adds anything beyond size, volatility, P&L so far and how close the account already was to its limit.

The plan was registered before any data was analysed: the OSF registration, timestamped {{ st.registered_on }}. The result is published whatever it shows, including a result that says the habits add nothing.

{%- elif v == "NOT MEASURED" %}

Not measured yet

After the exclusions, fewer than 100 accounts broke a rule, or fewer than 100 did not. That is too few for any verdict. Nothing is claimed either way, and the study keeps collecting under the same registration.

{%- elif v == "FAIL: does not predict usefully" %}

Result: the habit score did not predict breaches usefully

Accounts with higher DT-1 scores were not usefully more likely to break a loss rule in the next 30 days. Either the ranking accuracy (AUC) was below 0.55, or its whole 95% interval was below 0.60. donttrade does not offer DT-1 as a risk score. The habit report still describes past habits, with no claim about what happens next.

{%- elif v == "FAIL: adds nothing" %}

Result: habits rank risk, but add nothing beyond size, volatility and headroom

Accounts with higher DT-1 scores did break loss rules more often. But a simple model with no habits in it (activity, volatility, P&L so far and headroom) ranked them just as well, so the habit score added nothing to it. donttrade makes no claim that habits add predictive value.

{%- elif v == "PASS: meets target" %}

Result: in a pre-registered study, the habit score ranked later breaches, beyond size and volatility

Among accounts that lasted 30 trades, those with higher DT-1 scores broke a loss rule in the next 30 days more often. The score added to a model of activity, volatility, P&L so far and headroom, and its ranking accuracy reached the 0.65 target. This describes groups of past accounts. It does not say any one trader will break a rule.

{%- elif v == "PASS: below target" %}

Result: the habit score ranked later breaches, modestly

Among accounts that lasted 30 trades, those with higher DT-1 scores broke a loss rule in the next 30 days more often, and the score added to a model of activity, volatility, P&L so far and headroom. Its ranking accuracy stayed below the 0.65 target. donttrade describes it as a modest, group-level indicator for monitoring, not as a standard.

{%- elif v == "INCONCLUSIVE" %}

Result: inconclusive

The 95% interval was too wide either to pass or to fail. donttrade publishes this result and starts a replication on new accounts, under a new registration with DT-1 unchanged. The two results are never merged.

{%- endif %}

What this does not say

  • Nothing about any one trader. It describes groups of past accounts. No one is told they will break a rule.
  • DT-1 ranks accounts within a sample. It can’t yet score one new account on its own.
  • Past accounts only. Past results do not predict future results.

Check the code yourself

The firm runs the open-source script prediction_test.py on its own machine. The script has no network access and prints only group totals. Its SHA-256 fingerprints are listed in the registration, in the code’s licensing file and in the release {{ st.release_tag }}. Anyone can run it on the published synthetic file and compare the output byte for byte.

donttrade sells the donttrade test and gains from a positive result. That is why the plan was registered first, the code is public and every result is published. If anything here is found to be wrong, it is listed on corrections. Not investment advice.
